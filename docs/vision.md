# Well-plate detection with the overhead camera

This note covers the camera, the training data, the detector, the ROS guide node and their checks. The short version is in the README.

## Pieces

| Path | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| `src/gantry_description/worlds/lab_vision.sdf` | The lab world with a 96-well plate and a fixed overhead camera. Generated by `tools/make_vision_world.py`. |
| `vision/gantry_vision/camera.py` | Reads the camera's pose, field of view and image size from that SDF file, and projects 3D points the way Gazebo's camera does. |
| `vision/gantry_vision/plate.py` | Well and plate geometry in metres. The same layout as `gantry_control.protocol`, and a test checks they agree. |
| `vision/gantry_vision/render.py`, `dataset.py` | Draws the plate with lighting, clutter, noise, tilt and occlusion, and records exact labels. |
| `vision/gantry_vision/model.py` | `PlateNet`, a small PyTorch network with coordinate channels and about 1.1 million parameters. |
| `vision/gantry_vision/train.py` | Trains on fresh scenes each epoch, scores held-out scenes, exports ONNX and checks the ONNX output against PyTorch. |
| `vision/gantry_vision/inference.py` | Loads the ONNX file with onnxruntime and returns offsets, yaw and confidence. Does not import torch. |
| `src/gantry_control/gantry_control/plate_guide.py` | Decides between the camera's pose and the taught pose, then sends `RunProtocol`. |
| `src/gantry_control/scripts/plate_guide` | Command-line entry point for the guide. |
| `src/gantry_interfaces/action/RunProtocol.action` | Adds `offset_x_m`, `offset_y_m` and `yaw_rad`. They default to zero, so old commands still work. |

## What the detector predicts

The camera is fixed and looks straight down. The detector reads one 320 by 240 frame and predicts four numbers:

1. how far the plate has moved in X, in 10 mm units,
2. how far it has moved in Y, in 10 mm units,
3. how far it has turned, in 0.05 rad units,
4. a logit for "the plate is clearly visible". A sigmoid turns this into a confidence between 0 and 1.

The well centres are not predicted separately. The plate has a known layout, so the 96 centres follow from the pose. This keeps the output small and consistent with the protocol's geometry.

## Training data

Each epoch draws fresh scenes from a seeded generator. Each scene varies:

- plate offset up to 10 mm and turn up to 4 degrees, in the work plane;
- roll and pitch up to 3 degrees, which the detector does not predict and so must tolerate;
- overall brightness, a left-to-right and top-to-bottom light slope, colour tint, one specular highlight and sensor noise;
- up to three distractor squares on the bench;
- for 25% of scenes, the plate is absent or a grey box covers part of it. These are labelled "not clearly visible".

Held-out scenes use the same generator with a different seed and a plate range 1.4 times wider than training, so they test beyond the training range.

The renderer uses the same camera pose and lens as the Gazebo sensor. It is not Gazebo itself. Driving Gazebo's lighting and plate pose for thousands of frames would take far longer than the CI job allows. The Gazebo camera is still used for the live check in `simulation.yml`.

## The guide decision

`choose_pose` in `plate_guide.py` only trusts the camera when:

- the last five frames all arrived within two seconds,
- each has confidence of at least 0.8,
- each lies inside the protocol limit of 12 mm and 5 degrees,
- the five agree within 1.5 mm and 0.5 degrees.

Otherwise the run uses the taught pose, which is zero offset. The chosen reason is printed with every run. Frames are combined with the median so a single bad frame cannot move the plate.

## Limits in the protocol

`validate` rejects any plate move larger than 12 mm or 5 degrees, and rejects non-finite values. The bounds are checked before any motion starts, and the same validation runs again in replay.

## Checks

- `vision/tests`: camera projection against the SDF, label placement, dataset seeds, decoding and a tiny training run that compares ONNX with PyTorch.
- `src/gantry_control/test/test_plate_guide.py` and `test_protocol.py`: the decision rules, frame conversion and the new protocol limits.
- `vision.yml` CI job: runs all of the above, checks that the committed world matches the generator, then trains at full size and uploads `metrics.json` and the ONNX file.
- `simulation.yml`: builds the Docker image, starts the vision world, and checks that the live guide finds the camera-based pose.

Metrics are in `metrics.json` in the CI artifact `vision-evidence`. The numbers in the README come from that file.
